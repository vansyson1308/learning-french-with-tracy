## Limitations by design (not defects)

- The AI tutor seam ships **disabled**; no model, key or endpoint exists in
  the app, and the app makes no network requests of its own.
- No accounts, no sync: progress lives on the device; backup is a manual
  export/import file.
- **Vietnamese-native courses ship as reviewed or explicitly unreviewed
  (P3c review gate).** `fr-vi` compiles with `allowUnreviewed: true` in
  `content/courses/fr-vi.profile.json` because its Vietnamese text is still
  pending native review (entry above); the gate refuses any other
  Vietnamese-native course until a reviewer signs
  `review: { native, date }` or the owner records the same explicit
  exception here. `content/fr/REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md` (3,217 strings) is the
  reviewer's list for `fr-vi`.
- **`es-vi` (Spanish for Vietnamese speakers, P3d) ships unreviewed.** It
  compiles with `allowUnreviewed: true` in `content/courses/es-vi.profile.json`:
  its Spanish and Vietnamese text was authored with the P3c toolkit and has
  not yet been checked by a native Spanish teacher or a native Vietnamese
  reviewer. Since P3e the review is tiered: the owner signs
  `content/es/REVIEW-TIER1-vi.md` (the Vietnamese that teaches or judges) and a
  hired Spanish teacher checks `content/es/REVIEW-TIER1-es.md`; everything else
  is covered by the validators, the LanguageTool gate (CI job `languagetool`,
  allow-list with reasons in `content/es/languagetool-allow.json`) and the
  back-translation pass (`content/es/_drafts/BACKTRANSLATION.md`). Until the
  review is recorded the Learn tab labels the course "Bản beta — nội dung
  đang được duyệt" (`betaUntilReviewed`). The
  older `es-en` course is marked `"role": "standalone"`: it keeps its
  40 legacy lessons and its English dictionary but none of the new layers,
  and Spanish level claims and reports count only against `es-vi`.
- **DELE A1 practice (`es-vi`) reproduces the public 2020 format only.** Since
  P3e the track follows the 2020 model (four parts of 45 + 25 + 25 + 10 minutes,
  4 + 4 + 2 + 3 tasks with their formats including the lettered matching pools,
  two score groups — reading + writing and listening + speaking — each needing
  30 of 60 points; `trackVersion` 2), as described by the coordinator's spec
  and the references recorded in `content/es/exam/dele-a1.json`. The official
  Instituto Cervantes guide still could not be opened (2026-10-21 and
  2026-10-22: examenes.cervantes.es redirects to a maintenance page, the web
  archive is unreachable from this environment), so
  `formatSource.checkedAgainstOfficialGuide` stays `false` and the app says
  so. Writing and speaking are graded by the app's deterministic rubrics, so
  their points are coarse. Results are an estimated readiness, never a
  prediction of passing. Section practice draws on both mock forms, so a later
  mock may repeat questions already seen (the app says this).
- **Spanish audio is pending the generate run.** The 255 Spanish clips (lessons,
  checkpoints, exam, conversation partners) have scripts but no bundled audio
  until the P3b audio workflow generates them; listening items offer the
  audio-unavailable skip meanwhile, and the course pack uses device TTS for
  pack words (`audio.policy: device-tts`, reason recorded).
- **Audio for the non-French languages is produced by dispatched runs
  (P3b).** The multi-engine pipeline, the licence gate, the per-language
  canaries and the voice casts are in the repository; the canary runs for
  es/de/it/pt/ja/zh/ko/en/vi and the Spanish pack + lexicon generation run on
  GitHub-hosted runners after the merge and commit their results
  (`content/audio/voice-casts.json`, `content/reports/<lang>/audio-*.json`).
  Since P3e Korean is scored by character error rate (recognisers space
  Hangul arbitrarily) and one-word clips by an absolute duration band: ko now
  has one clean voice (MeloTTS-KR, mean CER 0.14), ja and zh have a verified
  second speaker (Kokoro `jm_kumo`, `zm_yunjian`), vi keeps one voice.
  Until a language's cast is `selected`, its inherited course keeps
  `audio.policy = device-tts`; a language with no clean voice is recorded as
  `no-clean-voice`, never voiced by a guessed model. Spanish pack audio is
  regenerated under pipelineVersion 2 (loudness-normalised, the canary's A
  voice) — its bytes change; French audio stays byte-identical.
- **Spanish variety (P3e).** `es-vi` models Peninsular Spanish
  (`content/es/VARIETY.md`); Latin-American forms (*boleto, jugo, carro,
  computadora, ustedes* for *vosotros*) are accepted in answers and shown as a
  note on the teach card, never taught as the model. Some recognition-only
  dictionary examples keep their Latin-American wording.
- **Daily new words (P3e).** Today adds up to N new syllabus words a day
  (Profile → Learning, 0–15, default 5). Spanish draws on a 613-lexeme A1
  syllabus; the French lexicon has 126 lexemes, so French daily words run out
  sooner (French content unchanged).
- **Lexicon frequency homographs (P3e).** The ETL splits a word form's count
  between every lemma sharing the form, which inflated rare verbs (*asir* from
  *así*, *casar* from *casa*). The 35 affected Spanish lexemes lost their
  frequency and left the syllabus (`content/es/_drafts/BACKTRANSLATION.md` §4);
  the ETL rule itself is unchanged, so new pools (de/it/pt) need the same check.
- **Spanish lexicon (P3a) is a derived draft.** `es-en` now ships an
  800-lexeme Spanish lexicon derived from Wiktionary (kaikki.org),
  FrequencyWords, wordfreq and Tatoeba (`content/es/lexicon/ACQUISITION.md`):
  English glosses come from Wiktionary senses (normalised, not reviewed),
  most Vietnamese glosses are `TODO:vi` drafts or bridged through the
  English Wiktionary translation table (listed in
  `content/es/lexicon/REVIEW.md`; a native review is the P3c handoff), and
  the inherited `es-en` course keeps its legacy scheduler
  (`content/courses/es-en.profile.json` disables FSRS / Today until P4a
  migrates learner data) — only the Vocabulary browser uses the new data.
- The seven non-French courses run on the legacy scheduler untouched; the
  French excellence layer is French-only (content, not code: since P1c the
  schema, validators and compiler accept any target language — see
  `content/README.md` — but only French has authored rich sources).
- Since P2b a target language can ship courses in several native languages
  (`fr-en` + `fr-vi`): the compiler emits one set of native-dependent
  artifacts per course (the primary native language — the smallest native
  code — keeps the historical `fr-*` names, a secondary one is prefixed by
  its course id, `fr-vi-*`) and shares the target-level ones (ids,
  attainment, claim, listening clips, pack audio). Two historical names are
  kept for golden byte-identity until a told rename: French reports stay at
  `content/reports/*.json` (other languages report under
  `content/reports/<code>/`) and the French writing artifact's token set
  stays under the `knownFrench` key.
- **Vietnamese translation reviewed by: pending native review.** The
  Vietnamese text of `fr-vi` (3,139 strings: glosses, example
  translations, options, prompts, questions, guidebooks, concept prose,
  can-do statements, instructions, cue facts, rubric slots, scenario goals)
  was drafted from the English source through the dictionary + JSONL
  workflow of `scripts/translate-native.ts` and has NOT yet been signed off
  by a native speaker. The review sheet is `content/fr/REVIEW-vi.md`
  (every string with its source; word banks and typed answers whose word
  order differs are flagged **re-author**). Until the owner records the
  review here, treat `fr-vi` wording as draft; the French target text,
  audio and grading are the same as `fr-en`.
- The A1 statement is a **CEFR-aligned A1 estimate** derived from the
  course's own checks; it is not an official or certified CEFR result and
  the assessment has no empirical validation (no response data, no item
  statistics, no standard setting) — `ASSESSMENT_DOSSIER.md`.
- No pronunciation scoring; spoken production is graded on what the
  recogniser transcribes.
- UI chrome is Vietnamese or English (P2a); course CONTENT is in the
  course's native language: English in `fr-en`, Vietnamese in `fr-vi`
  (P2b). A Vietnamese UI with `fr-en` selected therefore still shows
  English glosses and questions — switch to the course "explained in
  Tiếng Việt" from Profile → Learning → Switch. The seven inherited `*-en`
  packs remain English-only until their `-vi` courses ship (Phase 3/4).
- Learner history (cards, review log) lives in the learner store since
  persist v4: on a phone an expo-sqlite database (`learner-v4.db`), on the
  web tier a JSON document rewritten on every flush (its cost grows with the
  history — measured in `SOAK_REPORT.md`; the web tier is secondary).
  Memory holds only the active course's cards and its most recent 2,000
  review-log entries; screens that count cards (Profile *To review*) count
  the active course, as before.
- Web tier: the lexicon SQLite is replaced by a generated fallback; speech
  recognition is unavailable and the app says so.
