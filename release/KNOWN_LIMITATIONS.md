## Limitations by design (not defects)

- **Graded stories (P5b) exist for French (10), Spanish (20) and German (43) only.**
  Each further course from Vietnamese (it, pt, ja, zh, ko, en) gets its
  stories in its own milestone (P3d/P4c–P4f); the inherited `-en` packs get
  none. The French lexicon has 126 lexemes, so the French stories are all
  level 2 (the second half of the syllabus) and lean on a small vocabulary.
  Stories are original texts that have not had a native review yet
  (`plan/OWNER-FINAL-CHECKLIST.md` B).

- **Story audio: bundled clips or the device voice (QC 2026-10-29b §2.4).**
  A story whose sentences all have bundled clips plays them with the
  bundled player, unchanged. Every other story is declared device-voice:
  it is read along in the phone's own offline text-to-speech voice
  (expo-speech), sentence by sentence with the same highlight, labelled
  "Giọng đọc của máy" / "Device voice". This is never a silent fallback: a
  story with bundled clips never uses the device voice. With no offline
  voice for the language, the story is text only, with a one-line hint to
  install that voice in Android settings; the app makes no request and
  downloads nothing. Only voices judged on-device are used: Android voices
  named `…-network` and web voices with `localService: false` are skipped,
  and the chosen voice is passed by identifier. expo-speech exposes neither
  Android's `isNetworkConnectionRequired` nor its "not installed" feature,
  so the name rule and a speech error are the only signals. A voice listed
  but not downloaded switches to the hint when it fails to speak. Device
  voices differ in quality between phones and engines, and the story
  player has no slow mode (rate 0.9). Today every `de-vi` story is
  device-voice; `es-vi` and French stories are bundled.

- The AI tutor seam ships **disabled**; no model, key or endpoint exists in
  the app, and the app makes no network requests of its own.
- No accounts, no sync: progress lives on the device; backup is a manual
  export/import file.
- **Vietnamese-native courses ship as reviewed or explicitly unreviewed
  (P3c review gate).** `fr-vi` compiles with `allowUnreviewed: true` in
  `content/courses/fr-vi.profile.json` because its Vietnamese text is still
  pending native review (entry above); the gate refuses any other
  Vietnamese-native course until a reviewer signs
  `review: { native, date }` or the owner records the same explicit
  exception here. `content/fr/REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md` (3,217 strings) is the
  reviewer's list for `fr-vi`.
- **`es-vi` (Spanish for Vietnamese speakers, P3d) ships unreviewed.** It
  compiles with `allowUnreviewed: true` in `content/courses/es-vi.profile.json`:
  its Spanish and Vietnamese text was authored with the P3c toolkit and has
  not yet been checked by a native Spanish teacher or a native Vietnamese
  reviewer. Since P3e the review is tiered: the owner signs
  `content/es/REVIEW-TIER1-vi.md` (the Vietnamese that teaches or judges) and a
  hired Spanish teacher checks `content/es/REVIEW-TIER1-es.md`; everything else
  is covered by the validators, the LanguageTool gate (CI job `languagetool`,
  allow-list with reasons in `content/es/languagetool-allow.json`) and the
  back-translation pass (`content/es/_drafts/BACKTRANSLATION.md`). Until the
  review is recorded the Learn tab labels the course "Bản beta — nội dung
  đang được duyệt" (`betaUntilReviewed`). The
  older `es-en` course is marked `"role": "standalone"`: it keeps its
  40 legacy lessons and its English dictionary but none of the new layers,
  and Spanish level claims and reports count only against `es-vi`.
- **`de-vi` (German for Vietnamese speakers, P3d(de)) ships unreviewed**, on the
  same terms as `es-vi`: `allowUnreviewed: true` and `betaUntilReviewed` in
  `content/courses/de-vi.profile.json`. The owner signs
  `content/de/REVIEW-TIER1-vi.md` (378 lines) and a hired German teacher checks
  `content/de/REVIEW-TIER1-de.md` (309 lines). The rest is covered by:
  - the validators and the German gates (articles, plurals, capitals, dative
    chunks, verb-second and separable verbs, du/Sie register);
  - the LanguageTool gate (`content/de/languagetool-allow.json`);
  - the back-translation pass (`content/de/_drafts/BACKTRANSLATION.md`). The
    same session wrote both languages, so this pass is not independent.

  **German stories are text-only for now.** The 51 stories (40 at band A2-1,
  11 at A1-2) have no bundled audio. With it, German audio would be about
  17 MB against the 12 MB per-language budget. Story audio moves to
  per-language asset packs (`release/ASSET_DELIVERY.md`, QC 2026-10-29 §1.6);
  lesson words, syllabus words and listening clips are bundled.

  `de-en` is `"role": "standalone"`. Its lessons and stored surfaces are
  unchanged. Old progress maps to the German ids through `legacySurfaces` in
  persist v8, so no new migration was needed.
- **Content review is AI-simulated, not native (D-10, 2026-10-29).** The owner
  decided not to hire native reviewers. The hired-teacher step in the entries
  above is replaced by `plan/prompts/P-ai-review.md`:
  - separate reviewer agents with fresh context, answer keys first;
  - an independent 10 % audit with a 2 % P0+P1 bar;
  - each course's result in `content/<lang>/REVIEW-AI.md`.
  Results per course:
  - **`fr-vi`:** 81 findings on the Vietnamese layer. Audit: 1.4 %. The French
    text is the fr-en text, unchanged.
  - **`es-vi`:** first audit 2.5 %, over the bar, so the course was reviewed a
    second time and audited again (`content/es/REVIEW-AI.md`).
  - **`de-vi`:** 196 findings. Audit: 0.6 %.

  A course swaps its beta label for the honest note "Nội dung được AI kiểm duyệt
  kỹ, chưa qua giáo viên bản ngữ…" (Learn tab and Licences screen) only once the
  owner ticks its `ai-review:<course>` line in `plan/OWNER-FINAL-CHECKLIST.md` B.
  The app never says a native speaker reviewed it.

  Errors can remain: the audits are samples, and the reviewer and the author are
  both models. Fixes that need a word outside the course lexicon are recorded as
  "not applied", not forced in.
- **Basic packs (P4a).** `de-en`, `es-en`, `it-en`, `ja-en`, `ko-en`, `pt-en` and
  `zh-en` are the inherited Lingo Lessons packs (`"basicPack": true` in their
  profiles). Each has 5 units (es-en 10) of one 11-step template: recognition
  and translation into English only, no CEFR claim, no checkpoints, no listening
  or speaking layers, and the legacy SM-2 scheduler. it/ja/ko/zh have no bundled
  clips and speak through the device's text-to-speech. P4a repaired their data
  defects (whole-sentence and missing blanks, Korean spacing, Italian
  apostrophes, romanized "translations" accepted as answers, the German
  word-order note, words repeated across units) and added validator rules so
  these defects cannot return. Japanese, Korean and Chinese words now show their
  romanization (Profile → "Hiện phiên âm / Show reading aid", on by default).
  A basic pack leaves onboarding as soon as a Vietnamese course of the same
  target is A1-claimable. It stays under "Other courses", and no learner data
  moves. Today that applies to `es-en` (es-vi is A1-claimable). FSRS for the
  basic packs is not enabled (P4a task 5, optional): the packs have no
  stable-id registry, so the SM-2 → FSRS move would need one per language.
  They are retired course by course as the `-vi` courses ship instead.
- **DELE A1 practice (`es-vi`) reproduces the official 2020 format with original items.** Since
  P3e the track follows the 2020 model (four parts of 45 + 25 + 25 + 10 minutes,
  4 + 4 + 2 + 3 tasks with their formats including the lettered matching pools,
  two score groups — reading + writing and listening + speaking — each needing
  30 of 50 points; `trackVersion` 2), as described by the coordinator's spec
  and the references recorded in `content/es/exam/dele-a1.json`. The
  structure, durations, item counts, formats and the 30/50-per-group rule
  were checked against the two official documents (Guía de examen DELE A1
  v2020; Especificaciones DELE A1 2020) by the coordinator on the owner's
  behalf on 2026-10-22 (`checkedAgainstOfficialGuide: true`, `checkedBy`,
  `checkedAt`); each listening recording plays twice. Every item is
  original; the app is not affiliated with Instituto Cervantes. Writing and speaking are graded by the app's deterministic rubrics, so
  their points are coarse. Results are an estimated readiness, never a
  prediction of passing. Section practice draws on both mock forms, so a later
  mock may repeat questions already seen (the app says this).
- **Goethe A1 practice (`de-vi`) differs from the real exam in two places.** The track follows the Goethe-Institut
  Durchführungsbestimmungen (Stand 1 September 2025) and the Start Deutsch 1
  Modellsatz, both read on 2026-10-28 (`content/de/exam/FORMAT-SOURCES.md`).
  It keeps four parts of 20 + 25 + 20 + 15 minutes, 6 + 4 + 5 listening items,
  5 + 5 + 5 reading items and 2 writing tasks. Each part is worth 15 raw points
  × 1.66 (≈ 25), Schreiben is weighted 5 + 10 and Sprechen 3 + 6 + 6, and a
  pass needs 60 of 100.
  1. **Sprechen is a group exam** in the real test (up to four candidates, two
     examiners). The app simulates it one learner at a time and plays the
     partner and the examiner itself.
  2. **Teil 1 leaves out spelling a word and repeating a number.**

  Hören plays each recording as the format does: twice in Teil 1 and 3, once
  in Teil 2. A per-part `playsPerRecording` that the exam player enforces
  overrides the two plays of the product rule (§67).

  Every item is original. The app is not affiliated with the Goethe-Institut.
  Results are an estimated readiness, never a prediction of passing.
- **Spanish audio is bundled at 32 kbps (P3f, run 39761607493).** `es-vi` and
  `es-en` lesson audio, the 613 syllabus words and the 221 listening clips
  (Kokoro `ef_dora` / `em_alex`) total 7.4 MB; dictionary entries outside the
  syllabus and example sentences speak through the device voice. Five
  sentence clips are flagged for listening review in
  `content/reports/es/audio-quality.json`; playback of the 32 kbps files on a
  phone is part of the RC4 checklist (not yet observed).
- **Audio for the non-French languages is produced by dispatched runs
  (P3b).** The multi-engine pipeline, the licence gate, the per-language
  canaries and the voice casts are in the repository; the canary runs for
  es/de/it/pt/ja/zh/ko/en/vi and the Spanish pack + lexicon generation run on
  GitHub-hosted runners after the merge and commit their results
  (`content/audio/voice-casts.json`, `content/reports/<lang>/audio-*.json`).
  Since P3e Korean is scored by character error rate (recognisers space
  Hangul arbitrarily) and one-word clips by an absolute duration band: ko now
  has one clean voice (MeloTTS-KR, mean CER 0.14), ja and zh have a verified
  second speaker (Kokoro `jm_kumo`, `zm_yunjian`), vi keeps one voice.
  Until a language's cast is `selected`, its inherited course keeps
  `audio.policy = device-tts`; a language with no clean voice is recorded as
  `no-clean-voice`, never voiced by a guessed model. Spanish pack audio is
  regenerated under pipelineVersion 2 (loudness-normalised, the canary's A
  voice) — its bytes change; French audio stays byte-identical.
- **Spanish variety (P3e).** `es-vi` models Peninsular Spanish
  (`content/es/VARIETY.md`); Latin-American forms (*boleto, jugo, carro,
  computadora, ustedes* for *vosotros*) are accepted in answers and shown as a
  note on the teach card, never taught as the model. Some recognition-only
  dictionary examples keep their Latin-American wording.
- **Daily new words (P3e).** Today adds up to N new syllabus words a day
  (Profile → Learning, 0–15, default 5). Spanish draws on a 613-lexeme A1
  syllabus; the French lexicon has 126 lexemes, so French daily words run out
  sooner (French content unchanged).
- **Lexicon frequency homographs (P3e).** The ETL splits a word form's count
  between every lemma sharing the form, which inflated rare verbs (*asir* from
  *así*, *casar* from *casa*). The 35 affected Spanish lexemes lost their
  frequency and left the syllabus (`content/es/_drafts/BACKTRANSLATION.md` §4);
  the ETL rule itself is unchanged, so new pools (de/it/pt) need the same check.
- **Spanish lexicon (P3a) is a derived draft.** `es-en` now ships an
  800-lexeme Spanish lexicon derived from Wiktionary (kaikki.org),
  FrequencyWords, wordfreq and Tatoeba (`content/es/lexicon/ACQUISITION.md`):
  English glosses come from Wiktionary senses (normalised, not reviewed),
  most Vietnamese glosses are `TODO:vi` drafts or bridged through the
  English Wiktionary translation table (listed in
  `content/es/lexicon/REVIEW.md`; a native review is the P3c handoff), and
  the inherited `es-en` course keeps its legacy scheduler
  (`content/courses/es-en.profile.json` disables FSRS / Today until P4a
  migrates learner data) — only the Vocabulary browser uses the new data.
- The seven non-French courses run on the legacy scheduler untouched; the
  French excellence layer is French-only (content, not code: since P1c the
  schema, validators and compiler accept any target language — see
  `content/README.md` — but only French has authored rich sources).
