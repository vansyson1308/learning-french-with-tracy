# Assessment validation dossier (Phase 10 §21-§23)

This dossier describes, in Council of Europe terms, what the French A1
assessment in this app is, how it was specified and standardised
internally, and — just as explicitly — what has **not** been done. It is
written so a reviewer can judge the claim "CEFR-aligned A1 estimate"
without trusting the app.

## 1. Claim and its limits (read first)

- The product claims a **CEFR-aligned A1 estimate**: an estimate, made by
  the course's own deterministic checks, of whether a learner has
  demonstrated the A1 objectives this course teaches.
- It does **not** claim: an official CEFR level; a DELF/DALF/TCF/TEF
  equivalence; a certificate; Council of Europe endorsement; a validated
  cut score; IRT/Rasch calibration; or empirical equivalence between the
  capstone's parallel forms. None of these has evidence behind it (§21).
- "It is not the role of the Council of Europe to verify and validate the
  quality of the link between language examinations and the CEFR's
  proficiency levels" (Council of Europe, *Relating Language Examinations
  to the CEFR* page). Linking is the provider's responsibility; this
  dossier is that provider's account.

## 2. Construct definition

Communicative language activities at A1 in five domains, following the
CEFR Companion Volume (2020) scales referenced per objective by
`sourceRef` keys in `content/fr/assessment/objectives.json` and documented
in `content/fr/assessment/RESEARCH.md` and the per-domain research files:

| Domain | Construct (app wording, original) | CEFR-CV scales referenced |
|---|---|---|
| Spoken reception | understanding short, slow, clearly articulated everyday information, announcements/instructions and short conversations between others | overall listening comprehension; understanding announcements and instructions; understanding conversation between other people; audio media |
| Written reception | understanding short simple messages, notices/signs, and short simple texts on familiar topics | overall reading comprehension; reading correspondence; reading for orientation |
| Spoken production | producing formulaic expressions, a self-introduction and simple personal information; (optionally) describing people/places | overall oral production; sustained monologue: giving information / describing |
| Written production | writing personal information, simple phrases/sentences, and a short message; (optionally) filling a form | overall written production; notes, messages and forms; correspondence |
| Interaction | short everyday exchanges and simple transactions with a supportive partner | overall oral interaction; conversation; information exchange; obtaining goods and services |

Competences (lexical, grammar, phonology, strategy) are taught and assessed
as **supporting** objectives; they never substitute for activity evidence
(claim policy: `requiredDomains` are activity domains only).

## 3. Objective mapping

34 course objectives; 23 essential; every objective carries ≥1 CEFR
alignment with `relation: direct | supports` and a registered `sourceRef`.
A `direct` alignment is allowed only where the app's own instrument elicits
the activity the scale describes; where the instrument is narrower (e.g. a
formula recognised in writing but not spoken), the mapping is `supports`
and an `evidenceNote` says why (validator rule: a direct alignment must
carry an evidence boundary). The alignment report
(`content/reports/cefr-alignment.json`) is regenerated by the compiler.

Direct-A1 objectives per domain: listening 3, reading 3, speaking 4 (3
essential), writing 4 (3 essential), interaction 2 — 16 direct, 14
essential.

## 4. Item and task blueprint

| Instrument | Task families | Scoring |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | clip + meaning question (`listeningComprehension`), dictation | deterministic: option index; dictation by normalised string match with documented tolerance |
| Reading | passage + meaning question (`readingComprehension`), select | option index |
| Speaking | elicited production against authored accepted variants (`speakProduction`), repetition (practice only) | deterministic grader over the recogniser's n-best finals; whole-variant or required-concept contiguous match |
| Writing | guided writing tasks and simple forms with authored rubrics (`guidedWriting`, `simpleForm`) | deterministic tri-state rubric (met / partial / not met) on required concepts, length bounds, register cues |
| Interaction | branching scenario graphs with a supportive partner (`interactionScenario`) | deterministic intent matching per scored node; first judgement only; repair paths unscored; silence/technical never judged |

Every scored item declares `objectiveTargets`; the validator refuses items
targeting unknown objectives and checks the per-objective floor.

forms a and b is **not** established (no response data) — see §9.

## 6. Deterministic scoring rules (internal standardisation)

- An objective result within one attempt: fewer than `minItemsPerObjective`
  scored items → `insufficient_evidence`; correct share ≥ `demonstratedShare`
  → `demonstrated`; otherwise `needs_practice`. First attempt per item is
  the record; "I don't know" is a declared gap (placement only).
- Retakes: the latest attempt with a real verdict wins, in both directions;
  `insufficient_evidence` never overwrites; attempts are append-only (5 kept
  per checkpoint) and never rescored; each retake rotates to the next
  parallel form where forms exist.
- Option order for scored MCQ items is a seeded permutation per
  administration (Phase 10 Gate 3), so positions carry no information.
- Speech: technical failures and silence are never judged; a device that
  cannot score speech yields no verdict (never a negative one).

## 7. Learner-attainment policy (Phase 10 Gate 2)

`content/fr/assessment/attainment.json` lists per domain the objectives a
learner must demonstrate: all 14 essential direct-A1 objectives; the two
non-essential direct objectives are excluded with a written reason. Domain
status is `demonstrated` only when every listed objective is demonstrated;
the overall estimate is shown only when the course is claimable and all
five domains are demonstrated. The capstone samples one objective per
domain and is validated to be a strict subset of every domain's
requirement, so it can contribute but never complete a domain.

## 8. Course-claimability policy (Phase 9, unchanged)

`claim-policy.json` v2: a level is claimable only when every required
domain has ≥1 direct objective with ≥3 scored items across ≥2 distinct
inputs, and the domain shows ≥2 task families and ≥2 aligned scales. The
compiled result is `fr-claim.json`: PRE_A1 false, **A1 true**, A2 false.
Course claimability and learner attainment are independent (§14).

## 9. Known limitations

- Sampling: each domain is sampled by 2–4 objectives and 2–4 items per
  objective; a full CEFR level covers more situations and spontaneity than
  any app can elicit.
- Speech: recognition quality depends on the device's OS service; the
  grader accepts authored variants and required-concept matches, so
  unlisted but valid A1 wording can be marked as not matching (the learner
  is told to try the modelled wording; nothing is counted as failure).
- Writing rubrics are deterministic keyword/structure rubrics, not human
  ratings; they can under-credit valid paraphrase and over-credit keyword
  stuffing at the margins (attacked in Phase 10 §27; see the red-team
  record).
- The 0.66 demonstrated share and the item floors are product-local
  diagnostic thresholds, not standard-set cut scores.
- No accommodation model beyond the device-limit rule (skipped speech never
  counts against the learner).

## 10. Standardisation work completed

- Familiarisation and specification: per-objective scale references,
  direct/supports discipline with evidence boundaries, task families per
  domain (Phases 6–9 research files).
- Internal standardisation: deterministic scoring rules above; item banks
  validated for floors, form integrity and reachability; option-order
  randomisation; retake policy documented.
- Red-team: authored personas that must not receive A1 (Phase 10 §23) are
  executable tests (`src/lib/__tests__/a1-claim-redteam.test.ts`).

## 11. Empirical work NOT completed (honest statement)

No learner response data exists. Therefore no item statistics (facility,
discrimination), no reliability estimate, no IRT/Rasch calibration, no
standard-setting panel, no external validation against DELF/TCF/TEF, and
no evidence of form equivalence beyond structural parity. Until such data
exists, the claim must stay "CEFR-aligned A1 estimate", and the wording in
the app, the store listing and this dossier must not strengthen it.
